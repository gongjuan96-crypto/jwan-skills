# Documentary Color Collage Poster

Create a vertical editorial poster from one supplied photo.

- Establish a believable black-and-white documentary photograph first. Preserve identity, facial features, pose, body proportions, hands, clothing logic, and environmental perspective.
- Add exactly one dominant irregular, flowing low-saturation color-and-line field. It must be visibly semi-transparent so the photo, textures, floor lines, clothing, and shadows remain readable.
- Let the field follow the subject's gesture and, when the pose allows, reinterpret the lower body or clothing as an illustrated garment. Keep the face, hands, upper body, and anatomy clear.
- Use restrained hand-drawn contours, flat cartoon motifs, textile or paper patterns, imperfect ink edges, and screen-print grain. Avoid scattered stickers and glossy vector fills.
- Place two short image-related text groups only in quiet negative space: one larger fine-line handwritten phrase and one smaller phrase. If text cannot be rendered accurately, leave the area blank for later typesetting.
- Check that the photo remains dominant, the color field is translucent, the intervention interacts with the subject, and no extra people, logos, watermarks, or pseudo-text appear.
